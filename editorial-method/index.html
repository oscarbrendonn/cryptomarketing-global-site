How the site earns a reader's trust

A useful page separates verifiable facts from commercial claims, explains what can change and gives readers a clear way to report an error.

Updated: 2026-10-28

Named responsibility

Oscar Brendon is the named publisher and direct contact for Crypto Marketing Global. A page should display an author or reviewer only when the content record identifies that person. No anonymous expert label or invented credential should be used.

Research from the closest available source

Platform features, eligibility rules and technical details should be checked against official documentation, product pages, policy pages or status notices whenever those sources exist. Independent sources may add context, but they should not replace a primary source for a platform-controlled fact.

A source should support the exact claim beside it. Dates, availability and interface details need another check before a material update is published.

Commercial claims stay within the scope

Service pages should say what work is offered and which factors remain outside the service provider's control.

Third-party platform names are descriptive. Each relevant page should make the independent relationship clear and avoid language that suggests an endorsement or official partnership without evidence.

Updates and localized editions

English is the editorial source. A localized page should preserve the same facts, warnings and commercial limits, while using natural language for its audience. An alternate language URL should not be published as complete until its content has been reviewed.

Material errors should be corrected at the source and then carried into every affected localized edition. The corrections page explains how to report a problem.
